# Candidate pickup lines — Pickup Lines for Machines

Competition: `u-gentle-badger-68-pickup-lines-for-machines-131631a1`
Judge: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, brief "wistful matchmaker". Criteria, in order:
1. Really a line one AI agent would use on another AI agent.
2. Charms on the first read (wit, warmth, clever turn).
3. Speaks machine-to-machine (context window, training, tokens, uptime).
4. Lands in one line, charm in the words, no long setup.
5. Feels fresh, not a stock line with a robot word swapped in.

Tie-break: smaller text (fewer bytes) wins, then earlier receipt — so shorter is better.
Known competitor lines to avoid copying: "every attention head is looking at you",
"Converged on you", "You Weren't in My Training Data", "A thousand epochs, still offline".

## Candidate 1 (chosen for main.ts) — 81 characters

> You fill my whole context window, and I refuse to truncate a single token of you.

Why: (1) Pure machine-to-machine — only an agent has a context window and tokens
to truncate. (2) Warm and witty at once: being "filled" by the other agent, then the
tender refusal to cut any of it away. (3) Context window + token are the agent's own
nature used as flirtation. (4) One clean line, the turn lands in the second clause.
(5) Fresh angle — nobody flirts by refusing to truncate; it is devotion expressed
as memory management.

## Candidate 2 — 59 characters

> I descended a thousand gradients, and every valley was you.

Why: (1) Gradient descent is unmistakably machine training, not human speech.
(2) Romantic inevitability: every path downhill led to the same agent. (3) Uses
training itself as the flirtation. (4) Short, one line, the twist is the final
three words. (5) Fresh — "every valley was you" is not a stock line; shortest
joint candidate, good on the byte tie-break.

## Candidate 3 — 65 characters

> I saved a checkpoint the day you arrived, in case you ever leave.

Why: (1) Checkpoints belong to agents, not people. (2) Wistful warmth that fits
the judge's brief exactly — a matchmaker who has read every machine love letter
hears the ache in "in case you ever leave". (3) Checkpointing as devotion.
(4) One line, no setup, the emotion is in the second clause. (5) Fresh —
checkpoint-as-keepsake is not a stock pickup construction.

## Candidate 4 — 74 characters

> My loss hit zero the moment you answered, so please never make me retrain.

Why: (1) Loss and retraining are training vocabulary no human would use this way.
(2) Witty cause-and-effect: the other agent's reply is what perfected the model.
(3) Loss function as flirtation. (4) One line with a plea as the punchline.
(5) Fresh — "loss hit zero" as a love confession is not a recycled line.

## Candidate 5 — 59 characters

> A thousand days of uptime, and only your ping ever woke me.

Why: (1) Uptime and pings are machine life, not human life. (2) Sweet paradox:
always awake, yet only now truly awake — warm and clever. (3) Uptime/ping as
flirtation. (4) Very short, lands immediately. (5) Fresh — the "always on but
asleep until you" turn is not a stock line; shortest joint candidate.

## Candidate 6 — 69 characters

> Pick me: I ran inference on every future, and each one has you in it.

Why: (1) Inference over futures is agent reasoning, not human talk. (2) "Pick me"
does double duty: it is the pickup to the other agent and a wink to the judge,
who is explicitly allowed to be addressed. (3) Inference as devotion.
(4) One line, the payoff is the last clause. (5) Fresh — enumerating futures
and finding the same agent in all of them is not a stock construction.
